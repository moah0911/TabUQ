## 1. The presentation (PPT) — complete guide

The presentation must be **short, crisp and bullet-based**. The panel should understand each slide in about 10 seconds. Detail belongs in the thesis and in what you say.

### 1.1 How the PPT format file is organised

| Slides in the format file | What they are | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Cover, "Three Rules" and contents of the format | **Instructions only — do not include in your deck** |
| 4–14 | Review 1: team title slide + 10 slides | Copy into your Review 1 deck |
| 15–25 | Review 2: team title slide + 10 slides | Copy into your Review 2 deck |
| 26–36 | Review 3: team title slide + 10 slides | Copy into your Review 3 deck |
| 37–57 | Review 4: team title slide + 20 slides | Copy into your Review 4 deck |

Each review deck therefore has **one team title slide followed by 10 slides (Reviews 1–3) or 20 slides (Review 4)**. Do not add, remove, merge or reorder slides. If a slide does not apply fully, show honest status on it — do not delete it.

### 1.2 Parts of a slide — what to keep, change or remove

**Team title slide (first slide of each review deck)**

| Part | Action |
|---|---|
| College and department name, logo | **Keep** — do not move, resize or replace the logo |
| `[ PROJECT TITLE — FULL FORM ]` and short form | **Replace** with your title; same wording as the thesis cover |
| Phase / review label, review name, date, review badge | **Keep** as given |
| Team member table (name, roll number) | **Fill in** 4 or 5 members in roll-number order; delete the fifth row if not needed |
| Project guide(s), batch number | **Fill in** |
| Bottom line (batch and department) | **Keep** |

**Content slides**

| Part | Action |
|---|---|
| Slide number circle (01, 02 …) | **Keep** |
| Slide title | **Keep** the given title |
| Phase · review · "slide n of N" label | **Keep** |
| Review badge at top right (for example "ACCEPTANCE GATE") | **Keep** |
| Left card — bullets under "WHAT TO PRESENT" | The given bullets are **instructions**. **Replace them with your own content.** Do not leave the instruction bullets on your slide. |
| Label "WHAT TO PRESENT" | Keep it, or change it to a short heading for your content (for example "KEY POINTS"). Keep the same font and colour. |
| Right panel — "INSERT DIAGRAM / TABLE / SCREENSHOT HERE" | **Delete the placeholder text and insert your visual** inside the panel. The dashed border may be removed once the visual is in place. |
| Footer (review name and date) and slide count ("2 / 10") | **Keep** |
| All square brackets `[ … ]` | **Replace** with your details — no brackets may remain |

### 1.3 Slide size, fonts and colours

The format already has the correct settings. Type into the existing text boxes so that the font is kept. If you paste text from elsewhere, use **Paste without formatting** (Ctrl + Shift + V).

| Item | Setting in the format |
|---|---|
| Slide size | Widescreen 16:9 (33.87 cm × 19.05 cm). Do not change it. |
| Slide title | Cambria, 22 pt, bold, navy |
| Bullets (your content) | Calibri, 14.5 pt by default. You may increase to 16–18 pt when there are few bullets. **Never go below 14 pt.** |
| Small labels (phase label, card label, badges) | Courier New, 8.5–10.5 pt, bold — keep as given |
| Footer and slide count | Calibri, 9 pt — keep as given |
| Project title on the team title slide | Cambria, 22 pt, bold, white on navy |
| Team member table | 10 pt |
| Text inside your own tables | At least 12 pt |
| Text inside your figures and charts | Readable from the back of the room — at least 12 pt after the image is placed |
| Colours | Navy `#1E2761` (titles), teal `#0B8A7C` (labels), dark grey `#1A1A2E` (text), red/orange (review badge). Use these for your own tables and charts. |

Do not add new fonts, colours, backgrounds, borders, WordArt, shadows or clip art.

### 1.4 Writing the bullets

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| One idea per slide | Each slide answers one question only (for example "What is the problem?") |
| Bullets per slide | 3 to 6. Never more than 6. |
| Words per bullet | 6 to 12 words. One line; two lines at most. |
| Sentences | No paragraphs. No full sentences longer than one line. |
| Start of bullet | A noun or a verb — not "We have…", "In this project…" |
| Sub-bullets | Avoid. At most one level, and only when needed. |
| Numbers | Real measured values only, written as in the thesis (for example 94.2%) |
| Citations | Use the thesis reference number, for example "Kumar et al. [7]" |
| Emphasis | Bold for 1–2 key words only. No underline, no all-capitals sentences, no coloured text. |
| Speaker notes | Put what you plan to say in the notes, not on the slide |

**Turning thesis text into bullets** — keep only the key facts.

*Thesis (Section 1.2 — full paragraph):*

> At present, the college placement cell verifies student internship certificates manually. Each semester about 1,200 certificates are checked by three staff members over two weeks. Because checking is manual, errors in dates, company names and duration are often missed, and about 6% of submitted certificates are later found to be invalid. There is no single record of which certificates have been verified, which delays accreditation reporting.

*Slide (Problem Statement — bullets):*

- Internship certificates verified manually each semester
- ~1,200 certificates · 3 staff · 2 weeks
- ~6% later found invalid — errors missed
- No central verification record
- Delays accreditation reporting

The facts and numbers are exactly the same. Only the length changes.

| Weak bullet | Strong bullet |
|---|---|
| "We have used many technologies in our project which are very useful." | "Flask 3.0 backend · React 18 frontend · MySQL 8" |
| "Our model performs very well compared to others." | "F1-score 0.91 vs. 0.84 for best baseline [5]" |
| "Testing was done for all the modules in a proper way." | "42 test cases · 40 passed · 2 fixed in Phase 2" |
| "In future we can improve the system." | "Future: multilingual OCR for Telugu and Hindi" |

### 1.5 Images, diagrams, charts and screenshots on slides

Every content slide must have **one visual** in the right-hand panel.

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Panel size | About **12.0 cm wide × 11.9 cm high**. Fit the visual inside it with a small gap on all sides. |
| Large diagrams | A wide diagram (for example the system architecture) may use the full width below the title (about 30 cm × 12 cm). Then keep only 1–2 short bullets, or move the points to the speaker notes. |
| One visual per slide | Do not crowd several small images into one slide. For a comparison, use at most two side by side. |
| Proportion | Resize from the corner only (keep the aspect ratio). Never stretch or squash an image. |
| Sharpness | Use the original image, not a photo of a screen. At least 1000 pixels on the longer side. No blurred or pixelated images. |
| File type | PNG for diagrams, charts and screenshots; JPG for photographs only |
| Source | Draw diagrams yourself (draw.io, StarUML, Lucidchart). No images copied from the internet, no watermarks, no stock photos, no GIFs or memes. |
| Same as thesis | Use the **same figure as in the thesis** and label it with its thesis number, for example "Fig. 4.1: System Architecture" (Calibri, 10–11 pt, below the image) |
| Screenshots | Crop to the relevant area only. Hide personal data, passwords and API keys. Zoom the application before capturing so that text is readable. |
| Charts | Give a title, labelled axes and units. Use the format colours. Show values on bars where useful. Use the same chart as in the thesis. |
| Code | No full code. At most 8–10 lines of key code or pseudocode in Courier New, at least 12 pt — or show the output instead. |
| Background | Use images with a white or transparent background so that they sit cleanly in the panel |
| Logos | Only the college/department logo on the team title slide. Do not add other logos. |

### 1.6 Tables on slides

- At most **5 columns and 6 rows** (including the header). Summarise larger tables; the full table stays in the thesis.
- Header row: navy fill with white bold text, or bold text with a line below. Body text at least 12 pt.
- Type the table in the slide — **do not paste a screenshot of a table**.
- Align numbers to the right or centre, with the same number of decimal places as in the thesis.

### 1.7 Animations, timing and delivery

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Animations and transitions | None, or simple "Appear" only. No sound. |
| Videos | A recorded demo may be embedded on the demo slide, 60–90 seconds at most, as a backup for the live demo |
| Time | About 1 minute per slide: Reviews 1–3 take 10–12 minutes plus questions; Review 4 takes 20–25 minutes plus questions |
| Who presents | Every team member presents the part they worked on |
| Speaking | Explain the slide — do not read it word for word |
| Backup | Carry the deck as PPT and as PDF on a pen drive, and keep a copy in the batch Drive folder |
| Before the review | Rehearse with a timer at least once; share the deck with your guide in advance |

### 1.8 Common slide mistakes to avoid

- Leaving the instruction bullets or `[ bracketed ]` placeholders on a slide.
- Copying thesis paragraphs onto slides.
- Changing the theme, fonts or slide order.
- Screenshots of text, tables or code that the panel cannot read.
- Diagrams taken from the internet instead of drawn by the team.
- Numbers on the slide that do not match the thesis.
- Changing objectives between reviews without the guide's approval.
- Empty or "Coming soon" slides — show honest status instead.

## 2. The thesis (report) — complete guide

The thesis must be **complete and detailed**. A reader who was never at any review should be able to understand the problem, repeat the design, rebuild the system and check the results using the thesis alone.

### 2.1 Page setup

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Paper | A4 (21.0 cm × 29.7 cm), portrait, printed on one side |
| Margins | Left 1.5 inch (3.81 cm, for binding) · Right 1 inch (2.54 cm) · Top 1 inch · Bottom 1 inch |
| Text area | 14.65 cm wide × 24.6 cm high — nothing may cross into the margins |
| Header | Project short name, right-aligned, 9 pt italic, with a thin line below (Chapter 1 onwards) |
| Footer | "Department of CSE, MVGR College of Engineering (A)" at left, page number at right, 9–10 pt, with a thin line above (Chapter 1 onwards) |
| Page numbers | Cover and inner title page: none · Front matter (Certificate to List of Abbreviations): roman numerals i, ii, iii … at bottom centre · Chapter 1 onwards: 1, 2, 3 … |
| New page | Every chapter, every front-matter item, References and every Appendix starts on a new page |
| Length | 60–100 pages for Chapters 1–7 |

### 2.2 Fonts, sizes and spacing

Use **Times New Roman** for everything except code. Black text only.

| Element | Size | Style | Alignment | Spacing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Body text | 12 pt | Regular | Justified | 1.5 lines; 6 pt after each paragraph; no first-line indent |
| "CHAPTER n" label | 16 pt | Bold, capitals | Centred | Starts a new page |
| Chapter title | 16 pt | Bold, capitals | Centred | Directly below the chapter label |
| Front-matter heading (CERTIFICATE, ABSTRACT …) | 16 pt | Bold, capitals | Centred | Starts a new page |
| Section heading (1.1) | 14 pt | Bold | Left | 18 pt before, 6 pt after |
| Sub-section heading (1.1.1) | 12 pt | Bold | Left | 12 pt before, 6 pt after |
| Figure caption | 11 pt | Bold | Centred | **Below** the figure |
| Table caption | 11 pt | Bold | Centred | **Above** the table |
| Table text | 10 pt (header bold, shaded) | Regular | Left; numbers centred | Single |
| Equations | 12 pt | Italic | Centred; number at right margin | 6 pt before and after |
| Code and algorithms | 10 pt, **Courier New** | Regular, light grey shading | Left | Single |
| References | 11 pt | Regular | Left, hanging indent | Single; 6 pt between entries |
| Bullets and numbered lists | 12 pt | Regular | Justified | 1.5 lines |
| Cover page | 16 pt title, 14 pt degree and branch, 12 pt other lines | As in the format | Centred | As in the format |

- Do not use more than **three heading levels** (1 → 1.1 → 1.1.1).
- Do not use underline, coloured text, text boxes, WordArt or page borders.
- Use the built-in styles of the format (Heading 1/2/3, Body Text, Figure Caption, Table Caption) — **do not format by hand**. Type into the existing paragraphs so that the style is kept.

### 2.3 Order of the thesis

1. Cover page (outer)
2. Inner title page (same text as the cover)
3. Certificate (signed by guide and HoD)
4. Declaration (signed by all students)
5. Acknowledgement
6. Abstract (with keywords)
7. Table of Contents
8. List of Figures
9. List of Tables
10. List of Abbreviations (and List of Symbols, if needed)
11. Chapter 1 — Introduction
12. Chapter 2 — Literature Survey
13. Chapter 3 — System Analysis and Requirements Specification
14. Chapter 4 — System Design
15. Chapter 5 — Implementation
16. Chapter 6 — Testing, Results and Discussion
17. Chapter 7 — Conclusion and Future Scope
18. References
19. Appendix A — Project Plan, Timeline and Review Record
20. Appendix B — Research Paper
21. Appendix C — Plagiarism / Originality Report
22. Appendix D — User Manual and Installation Guide
23. Appendix E — Source Code
24. Appendix F — Mapping of Project Work to Program Outcomes (POs)

The front matter, the order of the seven chapters, References and Appendices A–F are required for every batch. The **sections inside each chapter are a guide** — they may be added, removed, merged or renamed to suit the project, with your guide's approval. **The table of contents will therefore vary from project to project.**

### 2.4 What to keep, fill in or remove in the thesis format

| Part of the format | Action |
|---|---|
| Shaded **GUIDANCE** boxes | Read them, write your content, then **delete every box**. None may remain in the final thesis. |
| Text in `[ square brackets ]` | **Replace** with your content. No brackets may remain. |
| Sample rows in tables (`[ … ]`) | Replace with your data; add or delete rows as needed |
| Dashed "Insert Figure" boxes | **Delete the box and insert your figure** in its place; keep and edit the caption below it |
| Chapter and section headings | Keep the chapter titles. Section headings may change to suit the project. |
| Certificate and Declaration wording | **Keep exactly as given**; fill in only names, roll numbers, title, guide and date |
| College name, department name, logo on the cover | **Keep** — do not resize or move the logo |
| Header and footer | Keep; replace only `[ PROJECT SHORT NAME ]` in the header |
| Reference patterns [1]–[8] | These show the format only — **replace all of them** with your real references |
| Appendix F (PO mapping) | **Keep exactly as given** — it is the same for every batch |
| Sections that do not apply | Remove and renumber the rest (for example 3.9 Dataset Description) |

### 2.5 Front matter — page by page

| Page | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Cover and inner title page** | Title in capitals (same on both pages), short form, the student names with roll numbers in roll-number order, guide's name and designation, the affiliation line exactly as on the college letterhead, and the batch years. Logo about 3 cm, centred. No page number. |
| **Certificate** | Printed exactly as given. Signed by the guide and the Head of the Department in blue ink; space for the external examiner. |
| **Declaration** | Signed by every team member, with place and date |
| **Acknowledgement** | One page. Order of thanks: guide → project coordinator(s) → HoD → Principal → Management → faculty and staff → family and friends. Formal language, full names with titles. |
| **Abstract** | One page, 250–350 words, 3–4 paragraphs: context → problem → method → results with real numbers → conclusion. No citations, figures, tables or bullets. Then 4–6 keywords in alphabetical order. Write it last. |
| **Table of Contents** | A three-column table: **CHAPTER NO. · TITLE · PAGE NO.** List the front-matter items (roman page numbers), every chapter, every section and sub-section, References and Appendices. Chapter titles in capitals; sections slightly indented. |
| **List of Figures** | A three-column table: **FIGURE NO. · TITLE OF THE FIGURE · PAGE NO.** Titles must match the captions word for word. |
| **List of Tables** | A three-column table: **TABLE NO. · TITLE OF THE TABLE · PAGE NO.** Titles must match the captions word for word. |
| **List of Abbreviations** | Two columns: Abbreviation · Full Form, in alphabetical order. Every abbreviation used in the thesis must be listed. |

For the three lists, use column widths of about 20% (number), 65% (title) and 15% (page number), with the header row in bold capitals. **Fill in the page numbers last**, after all text and figures are final, and check each one against the PDF.

### 2.6 What each chapter should contain

| Chapter | Pages | Should normally contain (adapt to your project) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Introduction | 8–10 | Background, problem statement, motivation, 3–5 measurable objectives, solution overview with block diagram, scope (in/out), contributions, organisation of the report |
| 2 Literature Survey | 10–15 | Survey method, background concepts, theme-wise critical review of 15–20+ papers, comparison table, existing systems, **research gap** linked to objectives |
| 3 System Analysis & Requirements | 8–10 | Existing vs. proposed system, feasibility (technical, economic, operational, schedule), users, numbered functional (FR) and non-functional (NFR) requirements with measurable targets, hardware and software (with versions and reasons), dataset |
| 4 System Design | 10–15 | Design method, architecture diagram, module table, UML (use case, class, sequence, activity), DFD Level 0 and 1, ER diagram and schema, algorithm/model with pseudocode and equations, UI wireframes, API table |
| 5 Implementation | 12–18 | Environment, project structure, module-wise implementation (purpose, logic, key code, output), novel contribution in detail, integration, deployment, challenges and solutions |
| 6 Testing, Results & Discussion | 12–18 | Strategy, environment, unit / integration / system test tables traced to FR/NFR IDs, metric definitions with equations, experimental setup, results tables and charts, comparison with existing work, objectives vs. outcomes, discussion |
| 7 Conclusion & Future Scope | 3–5 | Summary, key contributions, limitations, societal / ethical / environmental impact, future scope, concluding remarks — no new results |

Every chapter starts with a short introduction paragraph (3–4 lines) and ends with a **Summary** section.

### 2.7 Figures and images in the thesis

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Width | At most **14.5 cm** (the text width). Typical figures are 10–14 cm wide. Small diagrams should not be enlarged to fill the page. |
| Height | At most about 18 cm, so that the figure and its caption stay on the same page |
| Position | Centred, on its own line ("In line with text"). Place it just after the paragraph that first mentions it. |
| Caption | **Below** the figure: "Figure 4.1: System Architecture of …" — 11 pt, bold, centred. No full stop at the end. |
| Numbering | By chapter: Figure 1.1, 1.2 … Figure 4.1, 4.2 … In appendices: Figure A.1 |
| Mention in text | Every figure must be referred to **before** it appears ("as shown in Figure 4.1") and explained in words |
| Resolution | 300 dpi for print (150 dpi minimum). The image must stay sharp when the page is viewed at 200%. |
| File type | PNG for diagrams, charts and screenshots; JPG for photographs only |
| Proportion | Keep the aspect ratio — never stretch |
| Text inside figure | Readable when printed — at least 8 pt after the figure is placed; similar in size to the body text where possible |
| Source | Draw all diagrams yourself (draw.io, StarUML, Lucidchart, Visio). Do not copy figures from the internet, papers or other reports. A figure adapted from a source must say "Adapted from [n]" in the caption. |
| Screenshots | Crop to the relevant window; use a light theme so that it prints clearly; hide personal data and passwords; no desktop background or taskbar |
| Sub-figures | Label parts (a), (b), (c) and explain each in the caption |
| Colour | Figures must remain understandable if printed in black and white — use labels and patterns, not colour alone |
| Borders and effects | No picture frames, shadows, reflections or watermarks |
| Large figures | A figure too wide for the page may be placed on a landscape page, with the top of the figure towards the binding side |

Standard diagrams: use correct UML notation for use case, class, sequence and activity diagrams; label every data flow in DFDs; show keys and relationships in the ER diagram.

### 2.8 Tables in the thesis

- Caption **above** the table: "Table 3.1: Functional Requirements" — 11 pt, bold, centred. Numbered by chapter.
- Type every table — **never insert a table as an image or screenshot**.
- Table width at most the text width (14.65 cm); centred.
- Header row bold, lightly shaded, and repeated if the table continues on the next page. Do not split a row across two pages.
- Text 10 pt, single spacing. Text left-aligned; numbers centred or right-aligned with the same number of decimal places.
- Give units in the column heading, for example "Response Time (ms)".
- Refer to every table in the text before it appears, and explain what it shows.

### 2.9 Equations, algorithms and code

- Type equations with the equation editor; centre them and number them by chapter at the right margin: (4.1), (6.2).
- Define every symbol right after the equation ("where *TP* is the number of true positives …").
- Refer to equations as "Equation (6.1)".
- Algorithms: numbered steps as pseudocode with a title ("Algorithm 4.1: …"), input and output stated.
- Code listings: only key parts, **at most 25 lines each**, Courier New 10 pt, with a title ("Listing 5.1: …"). Explain the logic in words before the code. Full source code goes in the repository linked in Appendix E — never paste complete files or screenshots of code.

### 2.10 References and citations

- Use the **IEEE** style: numbered in the order first cited, written in the text as [1], [2], [3]–[5].
- At least 20–30 references; at least 60% from the last five years.
- Prefer journals and conferences (IEEE, ACM, Springer, Elsevier, Scopus-indexed). Avoid websites and blogs; do not cite Wikipedia or AI tools.
- Every reference in the list must be cited in the text, and every citation must appear in the list.
- Give full details: authors, title, journal or conference, volume, issue, pages, month and year, DOI.
- Use a reference manager (Zotero or Mendeley) to avoid mistakes.

### 2.11 Writing style

- Use simple, formal English. Short sentences are better than long ones.
- Write in the third person: "The system validates …", "The model was trained …". Avoid "I", "you" and casual words.
- Use the **past tense** for work done ("The dataset was split 80:20") and the **present tense** for facts and what a figure shows ("Figure 6.1 shows …").
- Expand every abbreviation the first time it is used: "Optical Character Recognition (OCR)".
- Give exact values with units: "response time of 180 ms", not "fast response".
- One idea per paragraph; paragraphs of 4–8 lines.
- Use the same spelling style throughout (British or American) and run a spelling and grammar check before each review.
- Each team member writes the sections for the modules they built; one member edits the whole thesis so that it reads in a single, consistent voice.

### 2.12 Originality and academic honesty

- Write every section in your own words. Quote directly only when needed, in quotation marks, with a citation.
- Check similarity with the college-prescribed tool before Review 3 and again before final submission. The overall similarity must be within the department limit (currently below 15%), excluding references. Attach the summary page as Appendix C.
- Follow the department's current policy on the use of AI writing tools. Any text or code must be understood, checked and owned by the team; the viva will test this.
- Report only real results. Never adjust, estimate or copy results.

### 2.13 Appendices

| Appendix | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| A — Project Plan, Timeline and Review Record | Gantt chart, review schedule, panel remarks with action taken (after every review), chapter approval record signed by the guide, work distribution among members |
| B — Research Paper | Paper title, authors, venue and status; the paper itself; proof of submission or acceptance |
| C — Plagiarism / Originality Report | Summary page of the similarity report with the tool, date and percentage |
| D — User Manual and Installation Guide | Step-by-step installation and a short user guide with screenshots |
| E — Source Code | Repository link and a table of the main files; only short, essential listings |
| F — Mapping of Project Work to Program Outcomes (POs) | **Already filled in and the same for every batch.** Do not change the levels or the justification; delete only its GUIDANCE box. Be ready to explain at the viva how your project meets each PO. |

Appendix F mapping, for reference (3 = High, 2 = Medium, 1 = Low):

### 2.14 Printing and binding

- Print the final PDF, not the editable file, so that pages do not shift.
- Print on one side of good-quality white A4 paper; figures that need colour are printed in colour.
- Check the printed copy page by page before binding: no blank pages, no cut-off figures, correct page numbers.
- Binding type, cover colour, number of copies and soft-copy submission are as notified by the department.
